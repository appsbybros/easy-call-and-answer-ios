APPS BY BROS · PRODUCT DECISIONS

Sell confidence,
not a settings menu.

The most valuable promise is “I can reach my people without confusion.” Reliability and successful setup matter more than adding another automation switch. The Android product already has paying customers; that is evidence of value, not evidence that every feature earns its complexity.

The market gives us a useful constraint

Apple already supplies Assistive Access calling with simplified controls and favorite contacts. Easy Call should complement that option for people who prefer to keep the normal iPhone, rather than implying Apple has no accessibility solution.

Simple Phone Book for Seniors markets large photo contacts, quick setup and a one-time purchase. That supports testing a focused, understandable purchase proposition. It does not prove our conversion rate or justify promising sales growth.

A realistic route toward 2–3×

Your figures—roughly 10 downloads daily and 2–3 one-time buyers monthly, plus some subscribers—are useful starting observations. They do not include enough cohort or revenue information to calculate the true conversion rate. As a rough illustration only, 300 new downloads and 3 purchases in the same month would be 1%; doubling or tripling those purchases would mean 6–9. Purchasers can come from older cohorts, so do not use that illustration as a measured baseline.

  1. First, remove uncertainty. On Android, ship and verify the call-control reliability work before spending more on acquisition. Ask users who experienced failures for device/Android version and timing, without requesting contact names or call recordings. Confirm the failure rate improved.
  2. Lead with the first minute. The new iOS app opens directly to adding a person. No account, permission barrage or payment wall before a useful action. A family member can help set up favorites without owning a separate account.
  3. Test a calmer offer. Calling and four favorites remain free. The 14-day extras preview begins deliberately, requires no payment details and never renews. A one-time $9.99 U.S. price is a starting proposal, not a configured price or guaranteed optimum. Existing saved people remain callable after expiry.
  4. Show the result in the first screenshots. “Your people, one clear place”; “Big controls, less searching”; “A gentle reminder to call.” Show the real app, not a fictional incoming-call screen. The iPhone listing must not inherit Android-only voice-answering or lock-screen claims.
  5. Speak to two audiences. One page speaks directly to an older person who wants independence; another speaks to an adult child setting up a parent's phone. Test one clear alternative against the baseline, not many small variants at once.
  6. Learn why people buy. Invite optional, short feedback after a meaningful period of use: easier dialing, helping family, larger text, reminders, Bluetooth, or another reason. Do not interrupt a call or demand a positive review. The existing Android trial-feedback work is worth preserving.

Measure enough to decide

Use store dashboards for impressions, installs, purchase proceeds, refunds and subscription retention. The new iOS app intentionally has no analytics SDK, so in-app activation is not silently tracked. Use opt-in interviews and observed setup sessions first. If consent-based diagnostic or aggregate analytics is later added, update the implementation and privacy disclosures first.

Track weekly cohorts over at least 6–8 weeks. With only a few monthly purchases, even a doubling can be noise. Prioritize 8–12 observed setup sessions with older adults and family helpers, then run a store experiment long enough for the console to report adequate confidence. Keep refund rate, support burden and call reliability as guardrails; revenue alone is not success.

Apple Product Page Optimization tests icons, screenshots and previews. Google Play listing experiments can test listing text and graphics. Neither tool guarantees an increase. Use one change per experiment where practical and avoid declaring a winner after a handful of sales.

Bluetooth is a promising niche, not the whole product

The subscriber who mentioned car use is a good interview lead. It is not yet evidence of a large segment. Ask what specifically failed in their previous setup: seeing the caller, routing audio, answering with a button, or voice commands. On iPhone, build on Siri, CarPlay and the vehicle's controls; never encourage tapping a custom phone screen while driving. Android's routing code deserves real accessory tests before stronger marketing claims.
